⚠️ Known Issue: Coach Retirement Lockout

If you ever retire your own coach, the game can permanently delete that coach and lock you out of controlling your own team.

This only happens to coaches currently marked as both user-created and user-controlled. Flipping both of those from True to False now - before anything goes wrong - prevents it completely. In testing so far, this has had no visible effect on a coach's stats, appearance, or how they play - but that's based on limited testing, not a guarantee about every possible long-term effect.

Run this every time you create a new coach - it only protects whichever coach is currently flagged, so a brand new coach you create later will need this run again.

You'll pick your save file, and a new, protected copy will be saved separately - your original file is never modified.

CFB27 Playoff Bracket Editor

Build a custom playoff bracket of any supported size, then write it directly into your save.
New here? Quick overview of how this works

1. Choose your save file below - this lets both tools read your teams' real records and compute their own ranking, independent of the game's own unreliable rank field.

2. If your own coach is at risk of a known retirement bug, a popup will show up automatically right after loading - just click the button in it, no extra steps needed. If it doesn't show up, your coach is already safe.

3. Check the Conferences tool above if your dynasty uses a different realignment than this tool's defaults - it can detect your real conference groupings automatically. Best done in Week 1 of Bowl Season, right after Conference Championships are actually played - too early and it can't crown champions yet. A red banner will also warn you here any time what's saved doesn't match what's actually in a save you load.

4. Switch to the Bracket Tool and pick your format (2, 4, 8, 12, or 16 teams).

5. Decide which conferences get automatic bids on the Autobids tab - the ranking engine and its tuning sliders live here too, if you want to adjust the methodology.

6. Fill in your bracket (At-Large seeds auto-fill by our ranking - override anything you want) and hit Apply. This writes a new save file - it never touches your original.

7. If your own team is in the bracket you just built: first switch control to a different team and make THEM Dynasty Owner, THEN retire your own coach, then bring your coach back. Doing it in this order matters - retiring while you're still Dynasty Owner yourself causes a separate problem. Do this after every single Apply, including both passes of a 16-team bracket.

8. Load that save in-game and continue - for 2-team, 4-team, and 8-team brackets, everything advances automatically from there, no need to come back to the tool. 16-team is the one exception: come back once Round 1 is actually played to reseed Round 2 (repeat step 7 after that Apply too).

    Ranking settings & computed rankings (used for autobids and At-Large fill)

    The game's own rankings only work for the actual real-life Top 25 — every other team's "rank" in the save file is basically random noise. So instead of trusting that, this tool builds its own ranking from scratch using things we can measure directly and reliably: who actually won each game. No guessing, no noise.

    Half the Score: Simulated Human Poll

    Stands in for what AP/Coaches voters would probably think of your team, based on things that actually matter: how often you win, how tough your opponents were, wins over strong teams, whether you're hot right now, and a boost for winning your conference.

    Half the Score: Computer Average

    Six different math formulas, each judging "how good is this team" a different way. Like the real BCS, each team's single best and single worst score among the six gets thrown out before averaging — so no one quirky formula can swing things too much.

    What do the 6 computer systems actually measure?
    RPI — Rewards beating good teams. Factors in your record, your opponents' records, and even your opponents' opponents' records.
    Colley Matrix — Pure wins and losses, nothing else. Good at sorting out similar records by who actually beat whom.
    Massey (simplified) — Solves every result at once like a big puzzle, to find each team's underlying strength. (The real version also uses final scores; we don't have those, so this is a win/loss-only approximation.)
    Elo — The same system chess uses. Win and your rating goes up (more for beating a strong team), lose and it goes down.
    SOS (Strength of Schedule) — How tough was your schedule overall? Beating good teams counts for more than beating bad ones.
    Wins Above Average — How many more wins did you get than a totally average team would have, playing your exact schedule?
    What are the small extra adjustments?
    Conference strength boost — A small bonus for playing in a conference that's tough top-to-bottom this season, not just your own individual schedule.
    Head-to-head tiebreaker — If two teams end up nearly tied and they played each other, whoever won that game gets the higher spot.
